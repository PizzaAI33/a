MINECRAFT BEDROCK • DEVELOPMENT BUILD 0.7.0

Foundry Arsenal

60 named weapons. Build your equipment from the ground up.

Validation status: Pack structure, references, gameplay math and script types are checked automatically. This build has not been imported or play-tested in Minecraft. First-person model alignment, held-fire input, station forms and natural ore placement require a client test. Target: Windows Bedrock 1.21.120 or newer, with stable Script API 2.3.0. No native mod loader.

Version 0.7 — field experience

Combat feedback: confirmed hits and headshots appear alongside the ammunition HUD; confirmed firearm defeats get a separate cue. Quiet sounds and HUD feedback can each be disabled in the Field Journal settings.

Practice targets: craft one at a normal crafting table with five oak planks, one redstone and one stick. Place the framed target, then shoot its printed face with ordinary firearm ammunition. Center hits score 10, the inner ring 7 and the outer ring 3. Edges score 1; the stand and narrow sides do not score. Use the target with an empty hand to see/reset your session score. Lifetime bullseyes remain. The streak counts consecutive bullseyes among target hits. Shotgun pellets score individually; explosive launchers do not score. New bunkers and the relic include targets.

Field tasks: Field Manual → Field journal/tasks → Field tasks/rewards. Six optional goals cover reloading, a workshop, attachments, practice, exploration and defending against hostile mobs. Claim each reward once. These tasks do not unlock or block recipes, and do not grant missiles or high-end cores.

Explore and return: operate a bunker switch or silo controller to remember the facility. The journal saves up to eight recent sites; select one for compass direction and distance in the HUD. Navigation does not teleport you or reveal undiscovered structures. Disable it whenever you prefer an uncluttered HUD.

Recycle equipment: Equipment → select weapon → Recycle weapon. Unload first, then confirm. Recycling returns one Steel Plate, one Polymer and the fitted attachments; the weapon is permanently consumed. Keep rare weapons you want to collect.

Design references: MrCrayfish’s Gun Mod informed readable combat/customization feedback, FTB Quests informed optional goals with bounded rewards, Waystones informed remembering visited places, and Immersive Engineering informed useful equipment in themed facilities. These are Java design references; no models, textures or dependencies from those mods are included. Minecraft client play-testing remains pending.

Retained 0.6 controls

Guns: the first-person rig now uses a single inverse arm transform and a normal bone hierarchy. Use fires; automatic weapons fire while Use is held and stop on release. Empty magazines reload from real matching ammunition in both Creative and Survival. Unloading returns the remaining rounds in both modes. Actual client hand alignment still needs verification.

Missiles: interact with a controller to claim/link it and receive a computer automatically. Its three choices are Target & launch, Load/Unload missile, and Maintenance/help. The computer has Aim & lock, Enter XYZ, and Launch. Confirming launch opens the cover, waits for it to finish, validates the shaft, then starts the ten-second countdown. Cancel is shown during preparation/countdown. Looking away never changes a locked target.

World generation: new relic sites survey every surface column in the full footprint. Water, lava, frozen oceans, low ground and steep terrain are rejected before placement; the search continues on another candidate. Existing silos are retained. New bunkers have three distinct relay, armory and expedition themes. The silo has a landing apron, striped cover walk, roof radar, equipment plant, lit buttresses and detailed service rooms.

Decorating: Facility Vent Grille, Hazard Stripe Panel, Facility Status Screen, Armored Facility Light and Relay Radar Dish are also craftable. Each uses one iron ingot, one redstone and one stone; lights/screens use glass instead of stone. Recipes yield four blocks, except the radar dish which yields one.

Upgrade: import both matching 0.7 packs. UUIDs are preserved and all manifest/dependency versions increase together. Test in a new world for the new sites and buildings; old structures remain. Chunk-loading commands and available ticking-area slots are still required for remote missiles. In-game rendering, held input, launch effects and multiplayer need a Bedrock test.

Install & start

  1. Open Foundry_Arsenal_60.mcaddon with Minecraft for Windows. Both packs should import.
  2. Create a new test world. Activate Foundry Arsenal | 0.7 under Behavior Packs. Verify the matching Resources 0.7 pack is active under Resource Packs.
  3. No experimental switches are intentionally required. Use a current retail Bedrock version. Operator commands need cheats. Remote silo placement and strikes also depend on Minecraft allowing script-issued ticking-area commands and having free ticking-area capacity; failure is shown in the targeting status.
  4. Enter the world. A Field Manual is given once per player. If needed, craft one from paper + copper ingot.
  5. For a quick creative test, use the Field Manual’s weapon catalogue to receive weapons and ammunition. Use an empty gun to reload it.
  6. Optional operator command: /function foundry/demo supplies five sample weapons, ammunition, attachments and grenades.

Bunkers & the relic silo — updated in 0.6

Three bunker variants generate sparsely in new Overworld land chunks. Search the entrances for sparse supplies and small ammunition finds; weapons and upgrades are rare. One relic silo site is reserved near the world's original spawn and placed once by the script, with one loaded Apex Nuclear ICBM. Read Field Manual → Bunkers, silos & missiles for its entrance coordinates and placement status. The missile never replenishes.

The reserved site is prepared using temporary chunk loading, so it does not require you to discover it first. If the game refuses chunk loading, it remains pending and the manual reports the problem. Bunkers only appear in newly generated chunks. The relic site may replace terrain or builds at its reserved location: start in a new test world.

Build your missile silo

  1. Craft a Missile Fabricator in a normal crafting table: top row diamond / redstone block / diamond; middle obsidian / furnace / obsidian; bottom iron block / iron block / iron block.
  2. Use the Fabricator for all silo parts, targeting tools and missiles. The full recipes are listed below. Apex requires two Stellar Cores, each containing a Nether Star, four Netherite Ingots and eight Echo Shards, plus additional costly modules.
  3. Build a full 5×5 Silo Casing foundation (25 blocks). Place a Silo Controller one block above its center. Put a Silo Stabilizer at each of the four corners, level with the controller.
  4. Leave a clear 3×3 shaft, 23 blocks high, directly above the controller. A visible missile appears above the loaded controller. Keep the sky path open for the best launch visual.
  5. Use the controller with an empty hand. Claim/bind the silo and receive its Field Targeting Computer; load a crafted missile. The first claimant controls loading and launch through the interface.

Choose and verify your target

Field Targeting Computer: equip it from your inventory and Use/right-click to open its interface. Enter XYZ directly or select Aim laser / lock new target. Aim at a surface and Use again to lock it; the computer then shows impact coordinates, chunk grid and your horizontal distance. Sneak + Use exits laser mode and returns to the computer. Laser range is 240 blocks; coordinate entry supports distant targets. The old Laser Designator still works for saved inventories. Y is the impact surface and controls crater depth.

Review the final launch confirmation, then a 10-second countdown starts. Cancel your own countdown from the pad before launch. Once launched, the strike continues even if you leave the area. One strategic missile operation can run at a time. This expansion works in the Overworld.

Apex detonation & terrain excavation

The Apex produces a flash, synthesized boom, expanding dust shockwave and rising particle mushroom cloud. Launch and descent use a stylized 3D missile with exhaust. Distant observers see a per-player projected particle effect in the direction of the real blast; it does not alter the real impact position. Apex now has an approximately 20-block-tall staged cylindrical silhouette, tapered nose and minimal base fins. The other three tiers scale down to approximately 5, 8 and 13 blocks. The cloud combines a growing world-space mesh with denser distant-view particles.

The largest crater is a 500-block-wide circular bowl, up to 100 blocks deep at its center, with progressively shallower edges. It is not a 500×500 square or a uniformly 100-deep cylinder. Terrain up to 80 blocks above impact is also cleared inside its circle. Bedrock and protected command/structure blocks remain, and world-height limits clip excavation. Lava, water, gravity and later game updates may alter the finished shape.

Excavation is permanent and runs in bounded, saved batches across temporarily loaded regions. Version 0.6 merges compatible cuts into rectangular fills, preloads the next region when an extra ticking-area slot is available, and raises the maximum work budget to 524,288 blocks / 512 fills per tick with a 10 ms work timer. This is a budget increase, not a measured 32× speedup. Actual completion depends on chunk generation and hardware; large strikes can still cause lag. The pad shows progress and any pause reason. Leaving and reopening the world resumes saved work. Fallout applies poison and weakness within 300 blocks for approximately two minutes of active simulation. No real radiation simulation is attempted.

Development build: these features have code and asset checks but no Minecraft client play-test. Test in a disposable world first. Natural placement, chest loot, missile animation, distant chunk loading and excavation need in-game validation. Do not treat a completed download as proof of client compatibility.

Optional operator supplies: /function foundry/siege_demo. This only gives equipment; it does not launch a missile.

Grenades & special effects

Fragmentation: splash damage. Smoke: a smoke cloud and temporary blindness inside it. Flash: line-of-sight blindness and a short white fade for players. Incendiary: burning cloud. Cryo: slowing cloud. Shock grenade: native blast with arc visuals. Shock firearm cores retain chaining. Explosive handheld grenades and rockets damage terrain and entities using native explosions. Smoke and flash are non-destructive. Rockets travel visibly; firearm bullets use immediate raycasts.

What this build does not claim

  • No completed in-game compatibility or multiplayer play-test.
  • Models are stylized cuboid interpretations with shared class construction and shared hold animations, not exact branded replicas. Shared rig families now animate recoil, the action/slide, magazine reload and support-hand motion; these are not 60 individually hand-authored animations.
  • No native R-key binding or working bipod deployment animation. Visible attachment changes currently cover optics only.
  • No real ballistic simulation, armor penetration simulation, homing handheld launchers.
  • Goggles and vest have functional equipment effects and icons; custom worn armor meshes are not included.

If something fails

Verify both packs are enabled and update Minecraft. For detailed errors, enable the content log in Minecraft's Creator settings. Send the version number and the first Foundry-related content-log error. An empty gun needs matching rounds and one Use action to reload. Close chat and release Use if a menu does not open. For new ores, start a new world or travel to unexplored chunks.
